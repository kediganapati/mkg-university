<a id="shloka1"></a>

**Shloka 2:1**

*तं तथा कृपयाविष्टमश्रुपूर्णाकुलेक्षणम् ।*

*विषीदन्तमिदं वाक्यमुवाच मधुसूदनः ॥ १ ॥*

*tam tatha kripayavishtam ashrupurnakulekshanam*

*vishidantam idam vakyam uvacha madhusudanah*

**Translation**

Sanjaya said: Seeing Arjuna overwhelmed with compassion, his eyes filled with tears and his mind deeply troubled by grief, Lord Krishna spoke these words to him.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:1**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita Chapter 2, Verse 1?

**Ganesh:** Sanjaya is telling Dhritarashtra that, seeing Arjuna overwhelmed with compassion, his eyes filled with tears and his mind deeply disturbed by grief, Lord Krishna spoke these words to him.

**Kedi:** In the middle of a battlefield, is Arjuna shedding tears a sign of weakness or some kind of flaw?

**Ganesh:** Shedding tears on the battlefield is a symbol of awakening. Arjuna had become deeply awakened; even before the war began, he could already see its consequences. Tears arise when one foresees an unjust outcome or when there is a strong possibility of such an outcome.

**Kedi:** Grief is usually felt only after losing someone. The war had not even started, so how could Arjuna already be grieving?

**Ganesh:** Grief is not felt only after someone is lost; it can also arise from the deep realization that someone is about to be lost. Arjuna had already become aware that after the war, he would have lost his own loved ones.

**Kedi:** Who is Madhusudana?

**Ganesh:** Madhu means attachment or delusion, and Sudana means destroyer. The nature that destroys unnecessary attachment is called Madhusudana. This is one of Lord Krishna's divine qualities, which is why Lord Krishna is also known as **Madhusudana**.

<a id="shloka2"></a>

**Shloka 2:2**

*श्रीभगवानुवाच ।*

*कुतस्त्वा कश्मलमिदं विषमे समुपस्थितम् ।*

*अनार्यजुष्टमस्वर्ग्यमकीर्तिकरमर्जुन ॥ २ ॥*

*shri bhagavan uvacha*

*kutas tva kashmalam idam vishame samupasthitam*

*anaryajushtam asvargyam akirtikaram arjuna*

**Translation**

The Blessed Lord said: O Arjuna, how has this weakness come upon you at such a critical moment? It is unworthy of a noble person, does not lead to heaven, and brings only disgrace.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:2**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 2?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna said to Arjuna, "O Arjuna, how has this weakness come upon you at such a critical moment? It does not befit a noble person. It leads neither to heaven nor to honor; it brings only disgrace."

**Kedi:** What is the connection between weakness and liberation (Moksha)?

**Ganesh:** Weakness prevents a person from performing their duties. Without performing one's actions, liberation (Moksha) cannot be attained. And one who fails to fulfill their responsibilities is certain to earn disgrace.

<a id="shloka3"></a>

**Shloka 2:3**

*क्लैब्यं मा स्म गमः पार्थ नैतत्त्वय्युपपद्यते ।*

*क्षुद्रं हृदयदौर्बल्यं त्यक्त्वोत्तिष्ठ परन्तप ॥ ३ ॥*

*klaibyam ma sma gamah partha naitat tvayy upapadyate*

*kshudram hridaya daurbalyam tyaktvottishtha parantapa*

**Translation**

O Partha, do not give in to weakness. It does not suit you. Cast aside this petty faint-heartedness and rise, O conqueror of enemies.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:3**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 3?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna said to Arjuna, "O Partha, do not give in to weakness. It does not suit you. Cast off this petty cowardice and rise, O conqueror of enemies."

**Kedi:** What is *Klaibyam*? Is it some kind of club?

**Ganesh:** Klaibyam means unmanliness or the refusal to live up to one's duty. When a man, woman, or any person turns away from their rightful responsibility, that behavior is described here as klaibyam. Arjuna was hesitating to perform his duty, so Lord Krishna urged him, "Do not display such weakness—fulfill your duty."

**Kedi:** What does it mean to give up cowardice?

**Ganesh:** Giving up cowardice means having the courage to do the very thing you are afraid of when it is the right thing to do.

<a id="shloka4"></a>

**Shloka 2:4**

*अर्जुन उवाच ।*

*कथं भीष्ममहं सङ्ख्ये द्रोणं च मधुसूदन ।*

*इषुभिः प्रतियोत्स्यामि पूजार्हावरिसूदन ॥ ४ ॥*

*arjuna uvacha*

*katham bhishmam aham sankhye dronam cha madhusudana*

*ishubhih pratiyotsyami pujarhau arisudana*

**Translation**

Arjuna said: O Madhusudana, how can I fight against Bhishma and Drona with my arrows in battle? They are both worthy of my highest respect.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:4**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 4?

**Ganesh:** Arjuna said to Lord Krishna, "O Madhusudana, how can I fight against Bhishma and Drona in battle with my arrows? Both of them are worthy of my highest respect and reverence."

**Kedi:** Arjuna seems to have a valid point. If someone is our elder, teacher, or role model, is it right to fight against them?

**Ganesh:** Respecting someone and agreeing with their beliefs are two different things. We can respect a person while disagreeing with their ideology. When an ideology stands against Dharma, it is right to oppose it, even if the person supporting it is highly respected or an ideal figure. Bhishma and Dronacharya stood in support of an unrighteous cause, so opposing them was the righteous course of action.

<a id="shloka5"></a>

**Shloka 2:5**

*गुरूनहत्वा हि महानुभावान् श्रेयो भोक्तं भैक्ष्यमपीह लोके ।*

*हत्वार्थकामांस्तु गुरूनिहैव भुञ्जीय भोगान् रुधिरप्रदिग्धान् ॥ ५ ॥*

*gurun ahatva hi mahanubhavan shreyo bhoktum bhaikshyam apiha loke*

*hatvartha kaman tu gurun ihaiva bhunjiya bhogan rudhira pradigdhan*

**Translation**

It would be better to live by begging than to kill these honorable teachers. If I were to kill them, every pleasure I enjoy would be stained with their blood.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:5**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 5?

**Ganesh:** Arjuna says to Lord Krishna, "It would be better to live by begging than to kill these honorable teachers. If I were to kill them, every pleasure I enjoyed afterward would be stained with their blood."

**Kedi:** What kind of comparison is this between killing and begging?

**Ganesh:** Arjuna is contrasting what he sees as two extreme choices. To him, killing his revered teachers would be the worst possible act, while living by begging would be the lowest way of earning a livelihood. He is saying that he is willing to accept the humblest life rather than commit what he believes to be the gravest wrong.

**Kedi:** Is killing really always the lowest kind of action?

**Ganesh:** According to the principle of karma, no action is good or bad in every situation. Taking a life to protect one's own life, innocent people, or one's country can be considered a righteous duty. But taking a life for selfish motives is regarded as one of the gravest sins, bringing severe negative consequences.

**Kedi:** Arjuna also had a point. How could anyone truly enjoy happiness after killing their own loved ones and teachers?

**Ganesh:** Arjuna was a Kshatriya. The duty of a Kshatriya is to uphold justice, not merely to think about personal happiness or sorrow. Arjuna should have focused not on the pleasure that victory might bring, but on the victory of justice itself.

<a id="shloka6"></a>

**Shloka 2:6**

*न चैतद्विद्मः कतरन्नो गरीयो यद्वा जयेम यदि वा नो जयेयुः ।*

*यानेव हत्वा न जिजीविषामः तेऽवस्थिताः प्रमुखे धार्तराष्ट्राः ॥ ६ ॥*

*na chaitad vidmah kataran no gariyo yad va jayema yadi va no jayeyuh*

*yan eva hatva na jijivishamah te avasthitah pramukhe dhartarashtrah*

**Translation**

We do not know which is better—to defeat them or to be defeated by them. The sons of Dhritarashtra stand before us, and after killing them, we would not wish to live.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:6**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 6?

**Ganesh:** Arjuna says to Lord Krishna, "We do not know which is better—for us to conquer them or for them to conquer us. The sons of Dhritarashtra stand before us, and after killing them, we would no longer wish to live."

**Kedi:** If Arjuna feels he would not want to live after killing them, then shouldn't he choose to die himself instead of killing others, so that at least their lives would be spared? Am I thinking correctly?

**Ganesh:** Not quite. Arjuna's confusion was making him see only two painful choices. Lord Krishna's teaching is that one should first understand and fulfill one's rightful duty. If, after performing one's duty, a person no longer wishes to live, it reflects that they have not yet understood the true nature of life and the Self. One who truly understands their existence learns to carry out their duty and then continue living with dignity, wisdom, and peace.

<a id="shloka13"></a>

**Shloka 2:13**

*देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा ।*

*तथा देहान्तरप्राप्तिर्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥ १३ ॥*

*dehino smin yatha dehe kaumaram yauvanam jara*

*tatha dehantara praptir dhiras tatra na muhyati*

**Translation**

Just as the embodied soul passes through childhood, youth, and old age in this body, it similarly passes into another body after death. A wise person is not bewildered by this.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:13**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Verse 2:13?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that just as the embodied mind passes through childhood, youth, and old age within this body, in the same way, after death, it moves into another body. A wise person is not disturbed by this.

**Kedi:** But the body is what allows the mind to experience different objects and pleasures. Naturally, one will become attached to this body, so the thought of this body being destroyed would surely be disturbing.

**Ganesh:** A more evolved body is always better than the present one. There are a total of 7 planetary realms and 1 Hiranyaloka, and each realm has its own more evolved bodily structure through which the mind experiences different objects and pleasures. If you remain attached to your current body, then how will you ever attain a more evolved body in the future? So always remember one thing: after death, your mind will certainly leave one body and enter another.

<a id="shloka18"></a>

**Shloka 2:18**

*अन्तवन्त इमे देहा नित्यस्योक्ताः शरीरिणः ।*

*अनाशिनोऽप्रमेयस्य तस्माद्युध्यस्व भारत ॥ १८ ॥*

*antavanta ime deha nityasyoktah sharirinah*

*anashino aprameyasya tasmad yudhyasva bharata*

**Translation**

The physical body is perishable, but the element of the self residing within the body is eternal, imperishable, and infinite. Therefore, O Bharata, perform your duty and fight.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:18**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Verse 2:18?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that the physical body is perishable, but the self element residing within the body is eternal, indestructible, and limitless. Therefore, O Bharata, perform your duty and fight.

**Kedi:** What is Sharirin?

**Ganesh:** The literal meaning of Sharirin is the master of the body. Within the body exists the Tripindi, which consists of three elements: the soul, the mind, and the life force (prana). Among these three, the mind is the master of the body. Therefore, the mind is called Sharirin.

**Kedi:** I saw in a TV serial that the soul was described as the master of the body. Isn't the soul the master of the body?

**Ganesh:** The soul and the life force within the body cannot make decisions by themselves. Only the mind makes decisions. If something cannot make its own decisions, how can it be called the master? The mind alone makes decisions, while the soul merely provides awareness and remains a silent witness.

**Kedi:** Is the mind really indestructible?

**Ganesh:** Yes. The human mind is indestructible and limitless. It continues to evolve endlessly and has no end. That is why Lord Krishna says not to worry about the destruction of the body. The body will perish, but your mind will not. Therefore, perform your duty.

**Kedi:** What does it mean that the mind is aprameya (immeasurable)?

**Ganesh:** The mind is made up of 108 elements: 64 emotions, 12 mental states, 12 personality traits, 8 states of consciousness, 8 aspects of willpower, and 4 mental perspectives. It is impossible to determine or measure which of these 108 elements is more active or inactive in any individual. Therefore, the mind is aprameya—it cannot be measured.

**Kedi:** Then what is the soul?

**Ganesh:** The soul is the same in all human beings and living creatures. The soul holds cosmic energy, which keeps the mind and the life force (prana) active. Through the soul, God is able to observe both the inner and outer actions of human beings, and through the soul, He sends thoughts related to one's actions into the intellect. Some monks, due to ignorance, mistake the mind for the soul and refer to the mind as if it were the soul.

<a id="shloka19"></a>

**Shloka 2:19**

*य एनं वेत्ति हन्तारं यश्चैनं मन्यते हतम् ।*

*उभौ तौ न विजानीतो नायं हन्ति न हन्यते ॥ १९ ॥*

*ya enam vetti hantaram yash chainam manyate hatam*

*ubhau tau na vijanito nayam hanti na hanyate*

**Translation**

One who thinks the mind kills, and one who thinks it can be killed, are both mistaken. The mind neither kills nor can it be killed.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:19**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Shloka 2:19?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that anyone who thinks the mind element kills someone, and anyone who thinks the mind element can be killed, are both mistaken. The mind neither kills anyone nor can it be killed.

**Kedi:** But when someone commits murder, they do it with their own mind. Then how is it possible to say that the mind element does not kill anyone?

**Ganesh:** When someone commits murder, they kill only the physical body. They cannot even touch the mind, and after the death of the present body, that mind takes on another body.

**Kedi:** But after death, that person's existence is also destroyed, isn't it? What about that?

**Ganesh:** Existence is destroyed more than a hundred times during life itself. Your existence has also been destroyed many times, and even today you are letting go of your old existence and taking on a new one by learning computers.

<a id="shloka20"></a>

**Shloka 2:20**

*न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन् नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।*

*अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ २० ॥*

*na jayate mriyate va kadachin nayam bhutva bhavita va na bhuyah*

*ajo nityah shashvato yam purano na hanyate hanyamane sharire*

**Translation**

The mind is never born and never dies. It has never come into being, nor will it ever cease to exist. It is unborn, eternal, everlasting, and ancient. It is not destroyed when the body is destroyed.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:20**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Shloka 2:20?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that the mind is never born and never dies. It has never come into existence, nor will it ever cease to exist. It is unborn, eternal, everlasting, and ancient. Even when the body is destroyed, it is not destroyed.

**Kedi:** If the mind is unborn and never comes into existence, then where did it begin? How did the mind exist for the very first time?

**Ganesh:** The human mind was originally a particle of God. Lord Ganapati separated the ego element from His own particle and gave it human form. To help that human mind evolve, He assigned it a journey through seven planetary realms and one Hiranya realm. When a human completes the journey through the seven planetary realms and the one Hiranya realm, fully developing the mind, that mind once again becomes a particle of God.

**Kedi:** Earlier you said that the mind's existence changes, but now you're saying that the mind's existence is eternal. What does that mean?

**Ganesh:** There are two kinds of existence: physical and spiritual. The mind's particle is its physical existence, which is eternal—only its form changes. The mind's behavior is its spiritual existence, which continuously lets go of one spiritual state and takes on another.

<a id="shloka47"></a>

**Shloka 2:47**

*कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।*

*मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ ४७ ॥*

*karmany eva adhikaras te ma phaleshu kadachana*

*ma karma phala hetur bhur ma te sango astu akarmani*

**Translation**

You have the right to perform your duty, but never to the fruits of your actions. Do not let the results be your motive, and never become attached to inaction.

**Commentary on Shloka 2:47**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2, Verse 47?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna tells Arjuna, "You have the right to perform your duty, but not to claim the fruits of your actions. Do not let the results become your motivation, and never become attached to inaction."

**Kedi:** People work because they expect results. I also work because I'll get paid. So what does it mean not to desire the fruits of action?

**Ganesh:** You may receive money after doing your work, but what ultimately happens because of that money is not entirely in your control. Sometimes your bike suddenly breaks down and the money is spent on repairs. Sometimes you fall ill and it goes toward medical expenses. Sometimes attachment leads you to buy unnecessary things. You spend money on food, travel, or business, but whether those expenses lead to profit or loss is not fully in your hands. Receiving money is not the final fruit; the real fruit is the outcome that follows from how it is used. Therefore, you should not become attached to the results that come after your actions. Instead, keep performing your duties with sincerity.

**Kedi:** What if I earn money but never spend it? Will I still receive the fruit of my actions?

**Ganesh:** Even the desire to hold on to money instead of spending it is itself a kind of fruit.

**Kedi:** Many times I've worked but never got paid. If I don't even receive the money, then how can there be any result from spending it?

**Ganesh:** The fruits of karma are of eight kinds: sensual fulfillment, purity, love, happiness, peace, knowledge, bliss, and liberation. People perform actions to attain one or more of these eight fruits. Money is only one possible means in life; experiencing any of these eight fruits is itself a form of the result of karma.

**Kedi:** So if I'm doing a job, I shouldn't keep thinking about whether I'll earn money from it or not. Whatever I do, the result will eventually come in the form of one of these eight fruits?

**Ganesh:** Not only should you avoid worrying about money, you should also avoid becoming attached to whether your work succeeds or fails. Complete your work honestly. If one effort fails, begin another. Do not waste your energy blaming others for failure.

**Kedi:** Who decides which fruit each person receives?

**Ganesh:** According to this teaching, Lord Vishnu and Goddess Lakshmi determine which fruit each person receives and at what time. Human beings are free to choose whether to perform good or bad actions, but they are not given the authority to choose the results of those actions.

**Kedi:** I've noticed that sometimes I do something with good intentions, yet I suffer a financial loss or even get physically injured while doing it. Is it possible that I was mistakenly given a bad result instead of the good result I deserved?

**Ganesh:** No one receives a bad fruit for a genuinely good action—even if that person's name is Kedi. If you experience hardship while doing good, this interpretation says it may be understood as divine grace: your past negative karma has been combined with your present good karma, reducing the severity of the negative consequences you might otherwise have faced.

<a id="shloka52"></a>

**Shloka 2:52**

*यदा ते मोहकलिलं बुद्धिर्व्यतितरिष्यति ।*

*तदा गन्तासि निर्वेदं श्रोतव्यस्य श्रुतस्य च ॥ ५२ ॥*

*yada te moha kalilam buddhir vyatitarishyati*

*tada gantasi nirvedam shrotavyasya shrutasya cha*

**Translation**

When your intellect crosses beyond the confusion of delusion, you will become indifferent to what has been heard and what is yet to be heard.

---

<a id="shloka53"></a>

**Shloka 2:53**

*श्रुतिविप्रतिपन्ना ते यदा स्थास्यति निश्चला ।*

*समाधावचला बुद्धिस्तदा योगमवाप्स्यसि ॥ ५३ ॥*

*shruti vipratipanna te yada sthasyati nishchala*

*samadhau achala buddhis tada yogam avapsyasi*

**Translation**

When your mind is no longer disturbed by conflicting ideas and becomes steady in deep meditation, you will attain yoga.

---

<a id="shloka54"></a>

**Shloka 2:54**

*अर्जुन उवाच ।*

*स्थितप्रज्ञस्य का भाषा समाधिस्थस्य केशव ।*

*स्थितधीः किं प्रभाषेत किमासीत व्रजेत किम् ॥ ५४ ॥*

*arjuna uvacha*

*sthita prajnasya ka bhasa samadhi sthasya keshava*

*sthita dhih kim prabhasheta kim asita vrajeta kim*

**Translation**

Arjuna said: O Keshava, what are the characteristics of a person whose wisdom is steady and who is established in meditation? How does such a person speak, sit, and walk?

---

<a id="shloka55"></a>

**Shloka 2:55**

*श्रीभगवानुवाच ।*

*प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् ।*

*आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥ ५५ ॥*

*shri bhagavan uvacha*

*prajahati yada kaman sarvan partha mano gatan*

*atmany eva atmana tushtah sthita prajnas tad uchyate*

**Translation**

The Blessed Lord said: O Partha, when a person gives up all selfish desires arising in the mind and finds complete satisfaction within the Self alone, that person is called one of steady wisdom.

---

<a id="shloka56"></a>

**Shloka 2:56**

*दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः ।*

*वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥ ५६ ॥*

*duhkheshu anudvigna manah sukheshu vigata sprihah*

*vita raga bhaya krodhah sthita dhir munir uchyate*

**Translation**

One whose mind remains undisturbed in sorrow, who is free from attachment in happiness, and who has overcome attachment, fear, and anger is called a sage of steady wisdom.

---

<a id="shloka57"></a>

**Shloka 2:57**

*यः सर्वत्रानभिस्नेहस्तत्तत्प्राप्य शुभाशुभम् ।*

*नाभिनन्दति न द्वेष्टि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ५७ ॥*

*yah sarvatra anabhisnehas tat tat prapya shubha ashubham*

*na abhinandati na dveshti tasya prajna pratishthita*

**Translation**

One who is free from attachment everywhere and neither rejoices nor hates when encountering good or bad fortune has steady wisdom.

---

<a id="shloka58"></a>

**Shloka 2:58**

*यदा संहरते चायं कूर्मोऽङ्गानीव सर्वशः ।*

*इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ५८ ॥*

*yada samharate cha ayam kurmo angani iva sarvashah*

*indriyani indriyarthebhyas tasya prajna pratishthita*

**Translation**

When a person withdraws the senses from their objects, just as a tortoise withdraws its limbs into its shell, that person's wisdom is firmly established.

---

<a id="shloka59"></a>

**Shloka 2:59**

*विषया विनिवर्तन्ते निराहारस्य देहिनः ।*

*रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते ॥ ५९ ॥*

*vishaya vinivartante niraharasya dehinah*

*rasa varjam raso apy asya param drishtva nivartate*

**Translation**

A person may give up sense pleasures by self-control, but the desire for them may still remain. That desire disappears only after realizing the Supreme Truth.

---

<a id="shloka60"></a>

**Shloka 2:60**

*यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः ।*

*इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥ ६० ॥*

*yatato hy api kaunteya purushasya vipashchitah*

*indriyani pramathini haranti prasabham manah*

**Translation**

O son of Kunti, even the mind of a wise person who strives for self-control can be forcefully carried away by the restless senses.

---

<a id="shloka61"></a>

**Shloka 2:61**

*तानि सर्वाणि संयम्य युक्त आसीत मत्परः ।*

*वशे हि यस्येन्द्रियाणि तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ६१ ॥*

*tani sarvani samyamya yukta asita mat parah*

*vashe hi yasya indriyani tasya prajna pratishthita*

**Translation**

Keeping all the senses under control and remaining devoted to Me, one whose senses are disciplined has steady wisdom.

---

<a id="shloka62"></a>

**Shloka 2:62**

*ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते ।*

*सङ्गात्संजायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥ ६२ ॥*

*dhyayato vishayan pumsah sangas teshu upajayate*

*sangat sanjayate kamah kamat krodho abhijayate*

**Translation**

When a person constantly thinks about sense objects, attachment develops. From attachment comes desire, and from desire arises anger.

---

<a id="shloka63"></a>

**Shloka 2:63**

*क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः ।*

*स्मृतिभ्रंशाद्बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥ ६३ ॥*

*krodhad bhavati sammohah sammohat smriti vibhramah*

*smriti bhramshad buddhi nasho buddhi nashat pranashyati*

**Translation**

From anger comes delusion, from delusion comes loss of memory, from loss of memory comes the destruction of wisdom, and when wisdom is destroyed, a person falls into ruin.

---

<a id="shloka64"></a>

**Shloka 2:64**

*रागद्वेषवियुक्तैस्तु विषयानिन्द्रियैश्चरन् ।*

*आत्मवश्यैर्विधेयात्मा प्रसादमधिगच्छति ॥ ६४ ॥*

*raga dvesha viyuktais tu vishayan indriyais charan*

*atma vashyaih vidheyatma prasadam adhigachchhati*

**Translation**

But one who is free from attachment and hatred, who keeps the senses under control, and who lives with self-discipline attains inner peace.

---

<a id="shloka65"></a>

**Shloka 2:65**

*प्रसादे सर्वदुःखानां हानिरस्योपजायते ।*

*प्रसन्नचेतसो ह्याशु बुद्धिः पर्यवतिष्ठते ॥ ६५ ॥*

*prasade sarva duhkhanam hanir asya upajayate*

*prasanna chetaso hi ashu buddhih paryavatishthate*

**Translation**

When the mind becomes peaceful, all sorrows come to an end. A calm and cheerful mind quickly develops steady wisdom.

---

<a id="shloka66"></a>

**Shloka 2:66**

*नास्ति बुद्धिरयुक्तस्य न चायुक्तस्य भावना ।*

*न चाभावयतः शान्तिरशान्तस्य कुतः सुखम् ॥ ६६ ॥*

*nasti buddhir ayuktasya na cha ayuktasya bhavana*

*na cha abhavayatah shantir ashantasya kutah sukham*

**Translation**

One who lacks self-control cannot attain steady wisdom or deep meditation. Without meditation there is no peace, and without peace there can be no lasting happiness.

---

<a id="shloka67"></a>

**Shloka 2:67**

*इन्द्रियाणां हि चरतां यन्मनोऽनुविधीयते ।*

*तदस्य हरति प्रज्ञां वायुर्नावमिवाम्भसि ॥ ६७ ॥*

*indriyanam hi charatam yan mano anuvidhiyate*

*tad asya harati prajnam vayur navam iva ambhasi*

**Translation**

When the mind follows the wandering senses, it carries away a person's wisdom, just as a strong wind sweeps a boat off its course.

---

<a id="shloka68"></a>

**Shloka 2:68**

*तस्माद्यस्य महाबाहो निगृहीतानि सर्वशः ।*

*इन्द्रियाणीन्द्रियार्थेभ्यस्तस्य प्रज्ञा प्रतिष्ठिता ॥ ६८ ॥*

*tasmad yasya mahabaho nigrihitani sarvashah*

*indriyani indriyarthebhyas tasya prajna pratishthita*

**Translation**

Therefore, O mighty-armed one, the person who has completely restrained the senses from their objects has steady wisdom.

---

<a id="shloka69"></a>

**Shloka 2:69**

*या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी ।*

*यस्यां जाग्रति भूतानि सा निशा पश्यतो मुनेः ॥ ६९ ॥*

*ya nisha sarva bhutanam tasyam jagarti samyami*

*yasyam jagrati bhutani sa nisha pashyato muneh*

**Translation**

What is night to ordinary people is the time of wakefulness for the self-controlled person. What ordinary people consider the time of activity is like night for the sage who sees the truth.

---

<a id="shloka70"></a>

**Shloka 2:70**

*आपूर्यमाणमचलप्रतिष्ठं समुद्रमापः प्रविशन्ति यद्वत् ।*

*तद्वत्कामा यं प्रविशन्ति सर्वे स शान्तिमाप्नोति न कामकामी ॥ ७० ॥*

*apuryamanam achala pratishtham samudram apah pravishanti yadvat*

*tadvat kamah yam pravishanti sarve sa shantim apnoti na kama kami*

**Translation**

Just as rivers continually flow into the ever-full and unmoving ocean without disturbing it, so too the person into whom desires enter without causing disturbance attains peace, not the one who constantly longs for desires.

---

<a id="shloka71"></a>

**Shloka 2:71**

*विहाय कामान्यः सर्वान्पुमांश्चरति निःस्पृहः ।*

*निर्ममो निरहङ्कारः स शान्तिमधिगच्छति ॥ ७१ ॥*

*vihaya kaman yah sarvan pumans charati nihsprihah*

*nirmamo nirahankarah sa shantim adhigachchhati*

**Translation**

One who gives up all selfish desires, lives free from craving, possessiveness, and ego, attains true peace.

---

<a id="shloka72"></a>

**Shloka 2:72**

*एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति ।*

*स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥ ७२ ॥*

*esha brahmi sthitih partha nainam prapya vimuhyati*

*sthitva asyam anta kale api brahma nirvanam richchhati*

**Translation**

O Partha, this is the state of spiritual realization. Having attained it, one is never again deluded. Remaining established in this state even at the time of death, one attains liberation and eternal union with the Supreme.
