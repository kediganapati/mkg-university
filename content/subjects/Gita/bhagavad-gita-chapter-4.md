**Shloka 4:1**

श्रीभगवानुवाच  
इमं विवस्वते योगं प्रोक्तवानहमव्ययम् ।  
विवस्वान्मनवे प्राह मनुरिक्ष्वाकवेऽब्रवीत् ॥ १ ॥

shri bhagavan uvacha  
imam vivasvate yogam proktavan aham avyayam  
vivasvan manave praha manur ikshvakave 'bravit

**Translation**

The Supreme Lord said: I taught this eternal science of Yoga to Vivasvan (the sun-god). Vivasvan taught it to Manu, and Manu in turn instructed Ikshvaku.

**Shloka 4:2**

एवं परम्पराप्राप्तमिमं राजर्षयो विदुः ।  
स कालेनेह महता योगो नष्टः परन्तप ॥ २ ॥

evam parampara-praptam imam rajarshayo viduh  
sa kaleneha mahata yogo nashtah parantapa

**Translation**

This supreme science was thus received through the chain of disciplic succession, and the saintly kings understood it in that way. But over the course of time, the succession was broken, and therefore this knowledge was lost.

**Shloka 4:7**

यदा यदा हि धर्मस्य ग्लानिर्भवति भारत ।  
अभ्युत्थानमधर्मस्य तदात्मानं सृजाम्यहम् ॥ ७ ॥

yada yada hi dharmasya glanir bhavati bharata  
abhyutthanam adharmasya tadatmanam srijamy aham

**Translation**

Whenever and wherever righteousness declines and unrighteousness rises, O Bharata, I manifest Myself.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:7**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Verse 4:7?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna told Arjuna, "O Bharata! Whenever and wherever there is a decline of Dharma and the rise of Adharma, at that time I manifest Myself."

**Kedi:** What does "I manifest Myself" mean? And before manifesting, where is the Lord absent?

**Ganesh:** The Lord resides in Vaikuntha. In Vaikuntha, there are seven divine realms, each inhabited by one Lord. Whenever Dharma declines, the Lord descends from Vaikuntha to Earth by taking a human body. After gaining sufficient experience and knowledge of human life on Earth, He reveals Himself.

**Kedi:** Does the Lord also need to gain experience and knowledge?

**Ganesh:** Every human being born on Earth needs to acquire knowledge and experience, and this rule also applies to the Lord because He has assumed a human body.

**Kedi:** Does Lord Shiva also enter a human body?

**Ganesh:** Shiva is not Bhagavan; He is Ishvara. Ishvara cannot enter a human body—only Bhagavan can take a human body.

**Kedi:** What is the difference between Bhagavan and Ishvara?

**Ganesh:** The word Bhagavan is made up of two words: Bhag and Van. Bhag means a human body, and Van means the one who possesses or bears it. Therefore, one who assumes a human body is called Bhagavan. Ish means control, and Var means the one who exercises it. The one who controls different dimensions is Ishvara. Shiva is Ishvara, while Shankara is Bhagavan.

**Kedi:** How many Bhagavans descend in human form?

**Ganesh:** There are a total of eight Bhagavans who incarnate in human form: Shankara, Dattatreya, Parashurama, Rama, Krishna, Buddha, Kalki, and Ganesh. Among them, Kalki and Ganesh are in the same body, while the other six have separate bodies.

**Kedi:** How do we know that Dharma has declined and the time has come for the Lord to descend to Earth?

**Ganesh:** When the entire world crosses the final limit of religious conversion, people abandon Dharma and turn toward Adharma so that they can commit Adharma in the name of a new religion, and only a few regions of India still preserve Dharma. When India also becomes a victim of religious conversion, then the Lord incarnates in India.

**Kedi:** Why does the Lord incarnate only in India? Why can't He incarnate in America or Korea?

**Ganesh:** The Lord incarnates on Earth with a particular nature, and that nature can be attained only in India. Every region gives human beings a distinct nature. In America and Korea, the influence of the material outlook is stronger, while the influence of the spiritual outlook is weaker. In India, the influence of the spiritual outlook is much stronger. Therefore, not only the Lord, but any person whose prarabdha includes spirituality is born in India.

**Shloka 4:8**

परित्राणाय साधूनां विनाशाय च दुष्कृताम् ।  
धर्मसंस्थापनार्थाय सम्भवामि युगे युगे ॥ ८ ॥

paritranaya sadhunam vinashaya cha dushkritam  
dharma-samsthapanarthaya sambhavami yuge yuge

**Translation**

To protect the righteous, to destroy the wicked, and to re-establish the principles of Dharma, I appear age after age.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:8**

**Kedi:** What is the meaning of Bhagavad Gita, Verse 4:8?

**Ganesh:** Lord Krishna told Arjuna that, to protect the righteous, destroy the wicked, and re-establish the principles of Dharma, I manifest Myself age after age.

**Kedi:** Which righteous people are being protected? I have seen 4–5 types of sadhus. Some were simply beggars, some were addicts, some practiced silence, and some were devoted to singing devotional hymns.

**Ganesh:** It is not referring to any of those sadhus. A sadhu means a person who is practicing the discipline of following Dharma; one who fulfills their responsibilities through a job, one who pursues their dreams through business, one who fulfills the responsibilities of family life by keeping their spouse happy, one who repays the debt owed to their parents by serving them, one who develops oneself through education, and anyone who upholds humanity while fulfilling their own responsibilities is truly a sadhu. It is that sadhu whom the Lord is referring to.

**Kedi:** How is that possible? I have a client who runs a business, uses intoxicants, gambles, borrows money from 3–4 friends to call escorts, and whenever he invites me for a meeting, he neither eats anything himself nor offers me anything to eat—he only gets intoxicated. How can he be a sadhu?

**Ganesh:** Merely taking responsibility does not make someone a sadhu. A sadhu is one who does not harm others, fulfills only one's own responsibilities, and follows humanity whether one performs worship and rituals or not.

**Kedi:** I understand who a sadhu is. Then who is the wicked person?

**Ganesh:** Those who adulterate food and other material goods, thereby damaging the health and livelihood of society; those who engage in corruption; those who betray their nation; those who commit legal crimes; those who accept bribes to pardon criminals; and those who loot the wealth of their country through corruption—all of them are wicked.

**Kedi:** What does it mean to re-establish Dharma?

**Ganesh:** When the principles of Sanatan Dharma are altered through adulteration and its original form is changed, Dharma begins to decline. By bringing those original principles of Sanatan Dharma to light again, spreading them, and re-establishing those original principles, Dharma is restored. This is called the re-establishment of Dharma.

**Kedi:** What does "age after age" mean?

**Ganesh:** Just as one year is the period in which the Earth completes one revolution around the Sun, similarly, the period in which the Sun completes one revolution around the black sphere named Krishna located at its center is called a Yuga. During one Yuga, the Earth completes 12,000 revolutions around the Sun. Therefore, one Yuga is 12,000 years long.

**Kedi:** I have heard of Satya Yuga, Treta Yuga, Dvapara Yuga, and Kali Yuga. What are they?

**Ganesh:** Just as the 12 months have 12 different names, similarly, the four Yugas in the Chatur Yuga have different names. After Satya Yuga comes Treta Yuga, after Treta Yuga comes Dvapara Yuga, after Dvapara Yuga comes Kali Yuga, and after Kali Yuga comes Satya Yuga. This cycle of time continues endlessly.

**Translation**

One who truly understands the divine nature of My birth and activities does not take birth again after leaving the body, but attains Me, O Arjuna.

**Shloka 4:29**

अपाने जुह्वति प्राणं प्राणेऽपानं तथापरे ।  
प्राणापानगती रुद्ध्वा प्राणायामपरायणाः ॥ २९ ॥

apane juhvati pranam prane 'panam tathapare  
pranapana-gati ruddhva pranayama-parayanah

**Translation**

Some offer the outgoing breath into the incoming breath, and others the incoming breath into the outgoing breath, practicing breath control (pranayama).

**Commentary on Shloka 4:29**

**Shloka 4:30**

अपरे नियताहाराः प्राणान्प्राणेषु जुह्वति ।  
सर्वेऽप्येते यज्ञविदो यज्ञक्षपितकल्मषाः ॥ ३० ॥

apare niyataharah pranan praneshu juhvati  
sarve 'py ete yajna-vido yajna-kshapita-kalmashah

**Translation**

Others regulate their diet and offer the life-airs into one another. All these practitioners understand the meaning of sacrifice, and through such sacrifices their sins are purified.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:30**

---

**Shloka 4:31**

यज्ञशिष्टामृतभुजो यान्ति ब्रह्म सनातनम् ।  
नायं लोकोऽस्त्ययज्ञस्य कुतोऽन्यः कुरुसत्तम ॥ ३१ ॥

yajna-shishtamrita-bhujo yanti brahma sanatanam  
nayam loko 'sty ayajnasya kuto 'nyah kuru-sattama

**Translation**

Those who partake of the nectar remaining after sacrifice attain the eternal Brahman. But for one who performs no sacrifice, even this world is not attainable, what to speak of the next, O best of the Kurus.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:31**

---

**Shloka 4:32**

एवं बहुविधा यज्ञा वितता ब्रह्मणो मुखे ।  
कर्मजान्विद्धि तान्सर्वानेवं ज्ञात्वा विमोक्ष्यसे ॥ ३२ ॥

evam bahu-vidha yajna vitata brahmano mukhe  
karmajan viddhi tan sarvan evam jnatva vimokshyase

**Translation**

Thus many kinds of sacrifices are described in the Vedas. Know that they all arise from action. Understanding this, you shall become liberated.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:32**

---

**Shloka 4:33**

श्रेयान्द्रव्यमयाद्यज्ञाज्ज्ञानयज्ञः परन्तप ।  
सर्वं कर्माखिलं पार्थ ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते ॥ ३३ ॥

shreyan dravyamayad yajnad jnana-yajnah parantapa  
sarvam karmakhilam partha jnane parisamapyate

**Translation**

O Parantapa, the sacrifice of knowledge is superior to the sacrifice of material possessions, because all actions ultimately culminate in knowledge.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:33**

---

**Shloka 4:34**

तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया ।  
उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥ ३४ ॥

tad viddhi pranipatena pariprashnena sevaya  
upadekshyanti te jnanam jnaninas tattva-darshinah

**Translation**

Learn the truth by approaching a spiritual teacher with humility, sincere inquiry, and service. The wise who have realized the truth will impart that knowledge to you.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:34**

**Shloka 4:35**

यज्ज्ञात्वा न पुनर्मोहमेवं यास्यसि पाण्डव ।  
येन भूतान्यशेषाणि द्रक्ष्यस्यात्मन्यथो मयि ॥ ३५ ॥

yaj jnatva na punar moham evam yasyasi pandava  
yena bhutany asheshani drakshyasy atmany atho mayi

**Translation**

Having attained this knowledge, O Pandava, you will never again fall into illusion. By it, you will see all beings within yourself and also within Me.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:35**

---

**Shloka 4:36**

अपि चेदसि पापेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यः पापकृत्तमः ।  
सर्वं ज्ञानप्लवेनैव वृजिनं सन्तरिष्यसि ॥ ३६ ॥

api ched asi papebhyah sarvebhyah papa-krittamah  
sarvam jnana-plavenaiva vrijinam santarishyasi

**Translation**

Even if you are the greatest sinner among all sinners, you can cross over the ocean of sin by the boat of knowledge.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:36**

**Shloka 4:37**

यथैधांसि समिद्धोऽग्निर्भस्मसात्कुरुतेऽर्जुन ।  
ज्ञानाग्निः सर्वकर्माणि भस्मसात्कुरुते तथा ॥ ३७ ॥

yathaidhamsi samiddho 'gnir bhasmasat kurute 'rjuna  
jnanagnih sarva-karmani bhasmasat kurute tatha

**Translation**

Just as a blazing fire reduces firewood to ashes, O Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge burns all karma to ashes.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:37**

**Shloka 4:38**

न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते ।  
तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति ॥ ३८ ॥

na hi jnanena sadrisham pavitram iha vidyate  
tat svayam yoga-samsiddhah kalenatmani vindati

**Translation**

In this world, nothing is as pure and purifying as knowledge. One who has attained perfection in Yoga gradually realizes this knowledge within the self.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:38**

---

**Shloka 4:39**

श्रद्धावान्लभते ज्ञानं तत्परः संयतेन्द्रियः ।  
ज्ञानं लब्ध्वा परां शान्तिमचिरेणाधिगच्छति ॥ ३९ ॥

shraddhavan labhate jnanam tat-parah samyatendriyah  
jnanam labdhva param shantim achirena adhigacchati

**Translation**

A person with faith, dedication, and control over the senses attains knowledge. Having gained knowledge, such a person quickly achieves supreme peace.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:39**

---

**Shloka 4:40**

अज्ञश्चाश्रद्दधानश्च संशयात्मा विनश्यति ।  
नायं लोकोऽस्ति न परो न सुखं संशयात्मनः ॥ ४० ॥

ajnash chashraddadhanas cha samshayatma vinashyati  
nayam loko 'sti na paro na sukham samshayatmanah

**Translation**

The ignorant, the faithless, and the doubting person are ruined. For one who is full of doubt, there is happiness neither in this world nor in the next.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:40**

---

**Shloka 4:41**

योगसंन्यस्तकर्माणं ज्ञानसंछिन्नसंशयम् ।  
आत्मवन्तं न कर्माणि निबध्नन्ति धनञ्जय ॥ ४१ ॥

yoga-sannyasta-karmanam jnana-sanchhinna-samshayam  
atmavantam na karmani nibadhnanti dhananjaya

**Translation**

O Dhananjaya, one who has renounced attachment to actions through Yoga, whose doubts have been cut away by knowledge, and who is self-controlled, is never bound by karma.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:41**

**Shloka 4:42**

तस्मादज्ञानसम्भूतं हृत्स्थं ज्ञानासिनात्मनः ।  
छित्त्वैनं संशयं योगमातिष्ठोत्तिष्ठ भारत ॥ ४२ ॥

tasmad ajnana-sambhutam hrit-stham jnanasinatmanah  
chhittvainam samshayam yogam atishthottishtha bharata

**Translation**

Therefore, with the sword of knowledge, cut away the doubt born of ignorance that dwells in your heart. Take refuge in Yoga, arise, O Bharata, and perform your duty.

**Commentary on Shloka 4:42**
