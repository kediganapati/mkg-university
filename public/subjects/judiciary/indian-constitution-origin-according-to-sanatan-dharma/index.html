Origin of the Constitution

Parmeshwar First Ganapati created a monarchical constitution for nation-building.

Incarnations of Bhagwan Shankar, Dattatreya, Parashurama, Ram, Krishna, Buddha, and Kalki established Sanatan Dharma and the constitution.

In Sanatan philosophy, there exists a concept of opposing forces — one that upholds Dharmic order, and another that promotes materialism and division.

Types of Constitutions

Feature Monarchy Democracy
Power Distribution 100% ruling ministers 75% ruling, 25% opposition
Opposition Rights None Can interfere in legislature and executive
National Planning Smooth, uninterrupted Disrupted by opposition, risk of treason
Citizen Unity Promotes patriotism Can foster inequality, casteism
Ministerial Accountability Immediate punishment for treason/corruption Often unpunished
National Objective Nation-first Personal or party gain, corruption

Pillars of the Monarchy Constitution

Three Pillars: Economics, Parliament, Judiciary.

Three Homes:

  1. Legislature — Parliament
  2. Executive — Government firms
  3. Judiciary — Courts

The constitution comprises 24 Sections — 8 each in Economics, Parliament, and Judiciary.

Ministerial Ratios

  • Economics: 1:1
  • Parliament: 1:2 — Minister and Deputy
  • Judiciary: 1:3 — Hear, Examine, Judge

Separation of Roles

Ministers and magistrates work only in the legislature.

Executive officers work in the executive home.

Executive magistrates work in the judiciary.

3.1 Economics Sections

  1. Manufacturing
  2. Agriculture-Husbandry
  3. Entertainment-Tourism
  4. Marketing-Broking
  5. Retail Services
  6. Government Firms
  7. Banking-Finance
  8. Import-Export

Ministers: 8

In addition:

  • Chief Minister of Economy per state
  • Prime Minister of Economy for the nation

3.2 Parliament Sections

  1. Education
  2. Development
  3. Property
  4. Defence
  5. Foreign
  6. Health
  7. Revenue
  8. Culture

Ministers: 8 Deputy Ministers: 8

In addition:

  • Chief Minister of Parliament per state
  • Prime Minister of Parliament for the nation

3.3 Judiciary Sections

  1. Domestic
  2. Social
  3. Cultural
  4. Government
  5. Industrial
  6. Prestige
  7. Executional
  8. International

Magistrates:

  • 8 for hearing
  • 8 for examining
  • 8 for judgment

In addition:

  • Chief Justice per state
  • Prime Justice for the nation

Fundamentals of the Monarchy Constitution

  • Single king, religious and educated, elected based on intelligence and contributions.
  • King serves for life; ministers may change but the king remains.
  • Citizens are divided into Original Citizenship and Refugee Citizenship.
  • Original citizens hold equal rights; refugee citizens’ rights are graded.
  • One identity card for all communities.
  • No caste-based privileges.
  • National governance may draw inspiration from India’s ancient civilizational wisdom while respecting the diversity of all communities.

Partition of the Nation

Area and population determine division.

The hierarchy flows as:

Village → Taluka → City → Metropolis → District → State → Nation → United Nation → Union Nation → World Nation

24 units combine to form higher levels — 24 villages form 1 taluka, and so on through the hierarchy.

Parliament of the Nation

There are four levels of parliament:

  1. Nation Parliament
  2. United Nation Parliament
  3. Union Nation Parliament
  4. World Nation Parliament

State Parliament

  • 8 Economic Ministers
  • 16 Parliament Ministers
  • 24 Magistrates

Speaker: Chief Ministers of Economy, Parliament, and Judiciary — 3

National Parliament

  • 3 Chief Ministers per state

Speaker: Prime Ministers of Economy, Parliament, and Judiciary + Prime Advisor + King — 5 prime humans

Currency of the Nation

There are four types of currency:

  1. Nation
  2. United Nation
  3. Union Nation
  4. World Nation

Currency must be eternal — bearing the image of Parmeshwar Ganapati.

One currency and one bank per nation; no private banks.

Currency is renewed every 18 years.

International trade rules govern United, Union, and World Nations.

Framework of the Constitution

Eight fundamental elements govern the constitutional framework.

Articles failing these elements must be amended or the constitution recreated.

  1. Citizenship and Rights
  2. Facilities and Reservations
  3. Currency and Property
  4. Religion and Culture
  5. Education System
  6. Justice System
  7. Electoral Process
  8. Economic Policy

8.1 Citizenship and Rights

There are two types:

  • Original Citizenship
  • Refugee Citizenship

Original citizens hold full rights in parliament, judiciary, and executive.

Refugee citizens’ rights are based on purpose; they may gain full citizenship upon fulfilling their duties.

8.2 Facilities and Reservations

Emergency aid is given based on need, not caste or religion.

Economic, intellectual, and physical reservations are granted individually, not on a caste basis.

8.3 Currency and Property

One currency and one property department.

Currency is renewed within 18 years.

Land and natural resources are national assets — no private ownership.

8.4 Religion and Culture

The government upholds the values of Dharma — truth, justice, and compassion — which are universal in nature.

8.5 Education System

Indigenous scientists and scholars are included in the curriculum.

Three pillars:

  1. Cultural schools
  2. Economic institutions
  3. Research universities

Education is free, with economic support and encouragement for invention.

8.6 Justice System

Eight courts:

  1. Domestic
  2. Social
  3. Cultural
  4. Government
  5. Industrial
  6. Prestige
  7. Executional
  8. International

Court decisions are final within each court.

National laws are decided in Parliament House.

8.7 Electoral Process

Jobs in parliament, executive, and judiciary are based on competence.

Selection is determined by:

  • Intellect
  • Physical strength, where needed
  • Mental stability

Voluntary service without salary is permitted as an exception.

8.8 Economic Policy

Balanced production and pricing of goods and services.

Categories of national industries and public-private sectors.

Tariff control and commodity markets.

International trade is based on fair currency or barter in cases of overvaluation.

Closing Words

This Gudi Padwa 2026, I am embarking on the creation of India’s new Constitution.

Writing this Constitution may take me around 5 years, and bringing it to life across India could take 10–20 years.
