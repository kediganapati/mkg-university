Ramayan — Bala Kanda — Sarga 04

Ramayan Bala Kanda

Verse 1

प्राप्तराज्यस्य रामस्य वाल्मीकिर्भगवानृषिः | चकार चरितं कृत्स्नं विचित्रपदमात्मवान् || १-४-१

The divine sage Valmiki composed the complete history of Rama after Rama had regained his kingdom, expressing it in varied and meaningful words.

Verse 2

चतुर् विंशत् सहस्राणि श्लोकानाम् उक्तवान् ऋषिः | तथा सर्ग शतान् पञ्च षट् काण्डानि तथा उत्तरम् || १-४-२

The sage composed twenty-four thousand shlokas, five hundred sargas, six Kandas, and also the later portion.

Verse 3

कृत्वा तु तन्महाप्राज्ञः स भविष्यम् सहोत्तरम् | चिन्तयामास कोन्वेतत् प्रयुञ्जीयादिति प्रभुः || १-४-३

Having composed that work, including future events and its later portion, the greatly wise sage considered who might properly present and preserve this epic.

Verse 4

तस्य चिन्तयामानस्य महर्षेर्भावितात्मनः | अगृह्णीतां ततः पादौ मुनिवेषौ कुशीलवौ || १-४-४

While that great sage of disciplined mind was reflecting, two boys, dressed as ascetics, came and touched his feet.

Verse 5

कुशीलवौ तु धर्मज्ञौ राजपुत्रौ यशस्विनौ | भ्रातरौ स्वरसम्पन्नौ ददर्शाश्रमवासिनौ || १-४-५

The sage saw the two brothers, Kusha and Lava, glorious royal sons, knowledgeable in Dharma, gifted with beautiful voices, and dwelling in the hermitage.

Verse 6

स तु मेधाविनौ दृष्ट्वा वेदेषु परिनिष्टितौ | वेदोपबृंहणार्थाय तावग्राहयत प्रभुः || १-४-६

Seeing the two intelligent boys, accomplished in the Vedas, the sage taught them the epic for the purpose of supporting and preserving Vedic knowledge.

Verse 7

काव्यं रामायणं कृत्स्नं सीतायाश्चरितं महत् | पौलस्त्यवधमित्येवं चकार चरितव्रतः || १-४-७

The sage, devoted to righteous conduct, composed the complete epic Ramayana—the great story of Sita and the destruction of the descendant of Pulastya, Ravana.

Verse 8

पाठ्ये गेये च मधुरं प्रमाणैस्त्रिभिरन्वितम् | जातिभिः सप्तभिर्युक्तं तन्त्रीलयसमन्वितम् || १-४-८

It was sweet both in recitation and song, arranged according to the three measures, connected with the seven musical modes, and accompanied by melody and rhythm.

Verse 9

रसैः शृङ्गारकरुणहास्यरौद्रभयानकैः | वीरादिभी रसैर्युक्तं काव्यमेतदगायताम् || १-४-९

The two sang this epic, endowed with the sentiments of love, compassion, humour, anger, fear, heroism, and other emotional expressions.

Verse 10

तौ तु गान्धर्वतत्त्वज्ञौ स्थानमूर्च्छनकोविदौ | भ्रातरौ स्वरसम्पन्नौ गन्धर्वाविव रूपिणौ || १-४-१०

The two brothers were skilled in the principles of music, knowledgeable in musical notes and modulation, gifted with excellent voices, and appeared like Gandharvas.

Verse 11

रूपलक्षणसम्पन्नौ मधुरस्वरभाषिणौ | बिम्बादिवोत्थितौ बिम्बौ रामदेहात्तथापरौ || १-४-११

Endowed with beautiful features and sweet voices, they appeared like two reflections arising from the very form of Rama.

Verse 12

तौ राजपुत्रौ कार्त्स्न्येन धर्म्यमाख्यानमुत्तमम् | वाचोविधेयं तत्सर्वं कृत्वा काव्यमनिन्दितौ || १-४-१२

Those two royal sons, flawless in conduct, mastered the entire excellent and righteous narrative and rendered the epic according to its proper expression.

Verse 13

ऋषीणां च द्विजातीनां साधूनां च समागमे | यत्र यत्र उपपन्नं तौ तत्र तत्रागायताम् || १-४-१३

Whenever gatherings of sages, learned Brahmanas, and righteous people took place, the two sang the epic there.

Verses 14–15

महात्मनौ महाभागौ सर्वलक्षणलक्षितौ | तौ कदाचित्समेतानामृषीणां भावितात्मनाम् | मध्ये सभं समीपस्थाविदं काव्यमगायताम् || १-४-१४–१५

Once, those two great and gifted souls, possessing every admirable quality, stood in the midst of an assembly of spiritually disciplined sages and sang this epic.

Verse 16

तच्छ्रुत्वा मुनयः सर्वे बाष्पपर्याकुलेक्षणाः | साधु साध्विति तावूचुः परं विस्मयमागताः || १-४-१६

Hearing it, all the sages, their eyes filled with tears, said, “Excellent! Excellent!” They were filled with great wonder.

Verse 17

ते प्रीतमनसः सर्वे मुनयो धर्मवत्सलाः | प्रशशंसुर्महात्मानौ गायमानौ कुशीलवौ || १-४-१७

All those sages, pleased in mind and devoted to Dharma, praised the great-souled Kusha and Lava as they sang.

Verses 18–26

The sages were deeply pleased by their performance and honoured the two brothers with various gifts. Some offered sacred articles, garments, vessels, seats, ornaments, and other objects. Others bestowed blessings upon them and praised the extraordinary epic composed by the sage.

The assembled sages recognised the Ramayana as a remarkable narrative, properly arranged and completed in its sequence.

Verse 27

आयुष्यमेतत् कीर्त्यम् च सर्वलोकप्रियम् शुभम् | सर्वपापप्रणाशं च कीर्तिमायुःप्रवर्धनम् || १-४-२७

This sacred narrative brings long life and fame, is beloved throughout the world, destroys all sins, and increases honour and longevity.

Verse 28

तद् गीतश्रवणैश्वर्यं तौ च गन्धर्वदर्शनौ | पथि सर्वत्र गायतौ श्रुत्वा रामोऽभ्यपूजयत् || १-४-२८

As the two, appearing like Gandharvas, sang everywhere along the roads, Rama heard their singing and honoured them.

Verses 29–30

स्ववेश्म चानीय ततो भ्रातरौ स कुशीलवौ | पूजयामास पूजार्हौ रामः शत्रुनिबर्हणः | आसीनः कांचने दिव्ये स च सिंहासने प्रभुः || १-४-२९–३०

Then Rama, destroyer of enemies, brought the two brothers, Kusha and Lava, to his own residence and honoured them, as they were worthy of honour. The lord Rama was seated upon his divine golden throne.

Verses 31–32

उपविष्टान् महात्मानौ काञ्चने परमासने | ददर्श रामो धर्मात्मा लक्ष्मणं च महाबलम् | भरतं च महात्मानं शत्रुघ्नं च महारथम् || १-४-३१–३२

The righteous Rama saw the two great-souled brothers seated upon an excellent golden seat. Lakshmana of great strength, the great-souled Bharata, and the mighty chariot-warrior Shatrughna were also present.

Verses 33–35

श्रूयतामेतदाख्यानमनयोर्देववर्चसोः | विचित्रार्थपदं सम्यग्गायकौ समचोदयत् | तौ चापि मधुरं रक्तं स्वचित्तायतनिःस्वनम् | तन्त्रीलयसमायुक्तं काव्यमगायतां तदा || १-४-३३–३५

Rama encouraged the two divine-looking singers, saying that this narrative, rich in varied meaning and expression, should be heard properly.

Then the two sang the epic with sweetness, emotional depth, a pleasing resonance, and harmony of melody and rhythm.

Verse 36

ततः तु तौ रामवचःप्रचोदितौ | अगायतां मार्गविधानसम्पदा | स चापि रामः परिषद्गतः शनैः | बुभूषयासक्तमना बभूव || १-४-३६

Thus encouraged by Rama’s words, the two sang according to the established method of musical performance. Rama, seated among the assembly, gradually became deeply attentive and absorbed in listening.
