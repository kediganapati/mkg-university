Ramayan — Bala Kanda — Sarga 18

Ramayan Bala Kanda

Verse 1

निर्वृत्ते तु क्रतौ तस्मिन् हयमेधे महात्मनः । प्रतिगृह्य सुरा भागान् प्रतिजग्मुर्यथागतम् ॥

When the great sacrifice, the Ashvamedha of the great-souled Dasharatha, was completed, the gods received their portions of the offerings and returned to their respective abodes.

Verse 2

समाप्तदीक्षानियमः पत्नीगणसमन्वितः । प्रविवेश पुरीं राजा सभृत्यबलवाहनः ॥

Having completed the vows and rules of consecration, the king entered the city together with his wives, servants, army, and vehicles.

Verse 3

यथार्हं पूजितास्तेन राज्ञा च पृथिवीश्वराः । मुदिताः प्रययुर्देशान् प्रणम्य मुनिपुङ्गवम् ॥

The other kings were duly honored by King Dasharatha. Delighted, they returned to their respective countries after bowing to the foremost of sages.

Verse 4

श्रीमतां गच्छतां तेषां स्वगृहाणि पुरात्ततः । बलानि राज्ञां शुभ्राणि प्रहृष्टानि चकाशिरे ॥

As those illustrious kings departed from the city for their own homes, their armies appeared bright, orderly, and joyful.

Verse 5

गतेषु पृथिवीशेषु राजा दशरथस्तदा । प्रविवेश पुरीं श्रीमान् पुरस्कृत्य द्विजोत्तमान् ॥

After the other rulers had departed, the illustrious King Dasharatha entered the city, preceded by the foremost Brahmanas.

Verse 6

शान्तया प्रययौ सार्धमृष्यशृङ्गः सुपूजितः । अन्वीयमानो राज्ञाथ सानुयात्रेण धीमता ॥

The greatly honored Rishyasringa departed together with Shanta, accompanied by the wise King Romapada and his followers.

Verse 7

एवं विसृज्य तान् सर्वान् राजा संपूर्णमानसः । उवास सुखितस्तत्र पुत्रोत्पत्तिं विचिन्तयन् ॥

Having thus sent all of them away, the king remained there happily, his heart fulfilled, thinking about the birth of his sons.

Verse 8

ततो यज्ञे समाप्ते तु ऋतूनां षट् समत्ययुः । ततः च द्वादशे मासे चैत्रे नावमिके तिथौ ॥

Then, after the completion of the sacrifice, six seasons passed. Thereafter, in the twelfth month, in the month of Chaitra, on the ninth lunar day—

Verse 9

नक्षत्रेऽदितिदैवत्ये स्वोच्चसंस्थेषु पञ्चसु । ग्रहेषु कर्कटे लग्ने वाक्पताविन्दुना सह ॥

When the constellation Punarvasu, whose deity is Aditi, was present, and when the five planets were in their exalted positions, with Cancer rising and Jupiter together with the Moon—

Verse 10

प्रोद्यमाने जगन्नाथं सर्वलोकनमस्कृतम् । कौसल्याजनयद्रामं सर्वलक्षणसंयुतम् ॥

At that auspicious time, Queen Kausalya gave birth to Rama, the Lord of the world, honored by all the worlds and endowed with all auspicious marks.

Verse 11

विष्णोरर्धं महाभागं पुत्रमैक्ष्वाकुनन्दनम् । लोहिताक्षं महाबाहुं रक्तौष्ठं दुन्दुभिस्वनम् ॥

He was greatly blessed, an embodiment of half of Vishnu, the son who delighted the Ikshvaku dynasty, red-eyed, mighty-armed, with red lips and a voice like the sound of a drum.

Verse 12

कौसल्या शुशुभे तेन पुत्रेणामिततेजसा । यथा वरेण देवानामदितिर्वज्रपाणिना ॥

Kausalya shone with that son of immeasurable radiance, just as Aditi shone with her son Indra, the foremost of the gods and wielder of the thunderbolt.

Verse 13

भरतो नाम कैकेय्यां जज्ञे सत्यपराक्रमः । साक्षाद्विष्णोश्चतुर्थांशः सर्वैः समुदितो गुणैः ॥

Kaikeyi gave birth to a son named Bharata, whose valor was founded upon truth, who was a fourth portion of Vishnu and endowed with all virtues.

Verse 14

अथ लक्ष्मणशत्रुघ्नौ सुमित्राजनयत्सुतौ । वीरौ सर्वास्त्रकुशलौ विष्णोरर्धसमन्वितौ ॥

Then Sumitra gave birth to two sons, Lakshmana and Shatrughna, both heroic, skilled in all weapons, and possessing an aspect of Vishnu’s essence.

Verse 15

पुष्ये जातस्तु भरतो मीनलग्ने प्रसन्नधीः । सार्पे जातौ तु सौमित्री कुब्जेऽभ्युदिते रवौ ॥

Bharata was born under the Pushya constellation with Pisces rising and with a serene mind. The two sons of Sumitra were born under the serpent constellation when the Sun had risen.

Verse 16

राज्ञः पुत्रा महात्मानश्चत्वारो जज्ञिरे पृथक् । गुणवन्तोऽनुरूपाश्च रुच्या प्रोष्टपदोपमाः ॥

Four great-souled sons were born to the king separately. They were virtuous, handsome, and radiant, resembling the stars of the Proshthapada constellation.

Verse 17

जगुः कलं च गन्धर्वा ननृतुश्चाप्सरोगणाः । देवदुन्दुभयो नेदुः पुष्पवृष्टिश्च खात्पतत् ॥

The Gandharvas sang melodiously, the groups of Apsaras danced, divine drums resounded, and a shower of flowers fell from the heavens.

Verse 18

उत्सवश्च महानासीदयोध्यायां जनाकुलः ॥

There was a great festival in Ayodhya, which was filled with people.

Verse 19

रथ्याश्च जनसम्बाधा नटनर्तकसंकुलाः । गायनैश्च विराविण्यो वादनैश्च तथापरैः ॥

The streets were crowded with people and filled with actors and dancers. They resounded with singers, musicians, and others engaged in celebration.

Verse 20

विरेजुर्विपुलास्तत्र सर्वरत्नसमन्विताः ॥

The broad streets shone with all kinds of precious jewels.

Verse 21

प्रदेयांश्च ददौ राजा सूतमागधवन्दिनाम् । ब्राह्मणेभ्यो ददौ वित्तं गोधनानि सहस्रशः ॥

The king gave appropriate gifts to the Sutas, Magadhas, and bards. To the Brahmanas he gave wealth and thousands of cows.

Verse 22

अतीत्यैकादशाहं तु नामकर्म तथाकरोत् । ज्येष्ठं रामं महात्मानं भरतं कैकयीसुतम् ॥

After eleven days had passed, the naming ceremony was performed. The eldest, the great-souled Rama, was named Rama, and Kaikeyi’s son was named Bharata.

Verse 23

सौमित्रिं लक्ष्मणमिति शत्रुघ्नमपरं तथा । वसिष्ठः परमप्रीतो नामानि कुरुते तदा ॥

Then Vashishta, greatly pleased, named the two sons of Sumitra Lakshmana and Shatrughna.

Verse 24

ब्राह्मणान् भोजयामास पौरान् जानपदानपि । उददद्ब्राह्मणानां च रत्नौघममलं बहु ॥

The king fed the Brahmanas, the citizens, and the people of the countryside. He also gave the Brahmanas abundant and pure collections of precious jewels.

Verse 25

तेषां जन्मक्रियादीनि सर्वकर्माण्यकारयत् । यथाकालं यथान्यायं नृपः पुत्रान् महात्मनः ॥

The king caused all the birth ceremonies and other rites of those great-souled princes to be performed at the proper times and according to the prescribed procedure.

Verse 26

तेषां केतुरिव ज्येष्ठो रामो रतिकरः पितुः । बभूव भूयो भूतानां स्वयम्भूरिव सम्मतः ॥

Among them, the eldest, Rama, became like a banner to his father and a source of delight. He became beloved among all beings like the self-born Brahma.

Verse 27

सर्वे वेदविदः शूराः सर्वे लोकहिते रताः । सर्वे ज्ञानोपसंपन्नाः सर्वे समुदिता गुणैः ॥

All four were learned in the Vedas, heroic, devoted to the welfare of the world, endowed with knowledge, and possessed of many virtues.

Verse 28

तेषामपि महातेजा रामः सत्यपराक्रमः । इष्टः सर्वस्य लोकस्य शशाङ्क इव निर्मलः ॥

Among them, the greatly radiant Rama, whose valor was founded upon truth, was dear to all the world like the spotless moon.

Verse 29

गजस्कन्धेऽश्वपृष्ठे च रथचर्यासु सम्मतः । धनुर्वेदे च निरतः पितुः शुश्रूषणे रतः ॥

Rama was accomplished in riding elephants, horses, and chariots. He was devoted to the science of archery and devoted to serving his father.

Verse 30

बाल्यात्प्रभृति सुस्निग्धो लक्ष्मणो लक्ष्मिवर्धनः । रामस्य लोकरामस्य भ्रातुर्ज्येष्ठस्य नित्यशः ॥

From childhood, Lakshmana, the enhancer of prosperity, was always deeply affectionate toward his elder brother Rama, who delighted the world.

Verse 31

सर्वप्रियकरस्तस्य रामस्यापि शरीरतः । लक्ष्मणो लक्ष्मिसंपन्नो बहिः प्राण इवापरः ॥

Lakshmana was dear to Rama in every way and served him with his whole being. He was like another life existing outside Rama’s own body.

Verse 32

न च तेन विना निद्रां लभते पुरुषोत्तमः । मृष्टमन्नमुपानीतमश्नाति न हि तं विना ॥

The foremost of men, Rama, did not sleep without Lakshmana, nor did he eat even delicious food brought before him without Lakshmana.

Verse 33

यदा हि हयमारूढो मृगयां याति राघवः । अथैनं पृष्ठतःऽभ्येति स धनुः परिपालयन् ॥

Whenever Raghava mounted a horse and went hunting, Lakshmana followed behind him, carrying and guarding his bow.

Verse 34

भरतस्यापि शत्रुघ्नो लक्ष्मणावरजो हि सः । प्राणैः प्रियतरं नित्यं तस्य चासीत्तथा प्रियः ॥

Shatrughna, the younger brother of Lakshmana, was always dear to Bharata, dearer to him than his own life; and Bharata was equally dear to Shatrughna.

Verse 35

स चतुर्भिर्महाभागैः पुत्रैर्दशरथः प्रियैः । बभूव परमप्रीतो देवैरिव पितामहः ॥

King Dasharatha became exceedingly delighted with his four greatly blessed and beloved sons, just as Brahma is delighted with the gods.

Verse 36

ते यदा ज्ञानसंपन्नाः सर्वैः समुदिता गुणैः । ह्रीमन्तः कीर्तिमन्तश्च सर्वज्ञा दीर्घदर्शिनः ॥

When the princes became endowed with knowledge and all virtues, modest, renowned, wise, and far-sighted—

Verse 37

ते चापि मनुजव्याघ्रा वैदिकाध्ययने रताः । पितृशुश्रूषणरता धनुर्वेदे च निष्ठिताः ॥

Those tigers among men were devoted to the study of the Vedas, devoted to serving their father, and accomplished in the science of archery.

Verse 38

अथ राजा दशरथस्तेषां दारक्रियां प्रति । चिन्तयामास धर्मात्मा सोपाध्यायः सबान्धवः ॥

Then the righteous King Dasharatha, together with his teachers and relatives, began to consider the marriages of his sons.

Verse 39

तस्य चिन्तयमानस्य मन्त्रिमध्ये महात्मनः । अभ्यागच्छन्महातेजा विश्वामित्रो महामुनिः ॥

While the great-souled king was considering this matter among his ministers, the greatly radiant sage Vishvamitra arrived.

Verse 40

स राज्ञो दर्शनाकाङ्क्षी द्वाराध्यक्षानुवाच ह । शीघ्रमाख्यात मां प्राप्तं कौशिकं गाधिनः सुतम् ॥

Desiring to see the king, Vishvamitra spoke to the doorkeepers: “Quickly inform the king that I, Kaushika, the son of Gadhi, have arrived.”

Verse 41

तच्छ्रुत्वा वचनं तस्य राज्ञो वेश्म प्रदुद्रुवुः । संभ्रान्तमनसः सर्वे तेन वाक्येन चोदिताः ॥

Hearing his words, all the doorkeepers, greatly attentive and agitated by his command, quickly rushed toward the king’s palace.

Verse 42

ते गत्वा राजभवनं विश्वामित्रमृषिं तदा । प्राप्तमावेदयामासुर्नृपायैक्ष्वाकवे तदा ॥

Having gone to the royal palace, they informed King Dasharatha of the Ikshvaku dynasty that the sage Vishvamitra had arrived.

Verse 43

तेषां तद्वचनं श्रुत्वा सपुरोधाः समाहितः । प्रतिजग्राह संहृष्टो ब्रह्माणमिव वासवः ॥

Hearing their words, Dasharatha, together with his royal priests, went forth with a composed mind and great delight, just as Indra goes toward Brahma.

Verse 44

तं दृष्ट्वा ज्वलितं दीप्त्या तपसं संशितव्रतम् । प्रहृष्टवदनो राजा ततोरघ्र्यमुपाहरत् ॥

Seeing the sage radiant with the brilliance of austerity and firm in his vows, the king, with a joyful face, offered him the traditional welcoming offering.

Verse 45

स राज्ञः प्रतिगृह्यार्घ्यं शास्त्रदृष्टेन कर्मणा । कुशलं चाव्ययं चैव पर्यपृच्छन्नराधिपम् ॥

Having received the offering from the king according to the prescribed custom, Vishvamitra asked the king about his well-being and welfare.

Verse 46

पुरे कोशे जनपदे बान्धवेषु सुहृत्सु च । कुशलं कौशिको राज्ञः पर्यपृच्छत्सुधार्मिकः ॥

The highly righteous Kaushika asked the king about the welfare of the city, treasury, countryside, relatives, and friends.

Verse 47

अपि ते संनताः सर्वे सामन्तरिपवो जिताः । दैवं च मानुषं चैव कर्म ते साध्वनुष्ठितम् ॥

“Are all the neighboring rulers submissive to you? Have your enemies been conquered? Are your religious and worldly duties being properly performed?”

Verse 48

वसिष्ठं च समागम्य कुशलं मुनिपुङ्गवः । ऋषींश्च तान् यथान्यायं महाभागानुवाच ह ॥

The foremost of sages, Vishvamitra, approached Vashishta and, according to proper custom, asked him and the other greatly blessed sages about their welfare.

Verse 49

ते सर्वे हृष्टमनसस्तस्य राज्ञो निवेशनम् । विविशुः पूजितास्तत्र निषेदुश्च यथार्हतः ॥

All of them, delighted at heart, entered the king’s residence after being duly honored and sat down according to their respective positions.

Verse 50

अथ हृष्टमना राजा विश्वामित्रं महामुनिम् । उवाच परमोदारो हृष्टस्तमभिपूजयन् ॥

Then the exceedingly generous king, delighted at heart, honored the great sage Vishvamitra and spoke to him with joy.

Verse 51

यथामृतस्य सम्प्राप्तिर्यथा वर्षमनूदके । यथा सदृशदारेषु पुत्रजन्माप्रजस्य च ॥

“I regard your arrival as being like the attainment of nectar, like rain falling upon a land without water, and like the birth of a son to one who has been without children and has a worthy wife.”

Verse 52

प्रणष्टस्य यथालाभो यथा हर्षो महोदये । तथैवागमनं मन्ये स्वागतं ते महामुने ॥

“Just as the recovery of something lost and the joy that comes from great prosperity bring happiness, so do I regard your arrival. O great sage, you are most welcome.”

Verse 53

कं च ते परमं कामं करोमि किमु हर्षितः । पात्रभूतोऽसि मे ब्रह्मन् दिष्ट्या प्राप्तोऽसि मानद ॥

“What is the supreme desire of yours that I should fulfill? Why should I not be delighted? O Brahman, you are worthy of receiving from me. O giver of honor, fortunately you have come.”

Verse 54

अद्य मे सफलं जन्म जीवितं च सुजीवितम् । यस्माद्विप्रेन्द्रमद्राक्षं सुप्रभाता निशा मम ॥

“Today my birth has become fruitful and my life has become well lived, because I have seen the foremost of Brahmanas. Therefore, this night of mine has become truly auspicious.”

Verse 55

पूर्वं राजर्षिशब्देन तपसा द्योतितप्रभः । ब्रह्मर्षित्वमनुप्राप्तः पूज्योऽसि बहुधा मया ॥

“Formerly, you were renowned by the title of Rajarshi, your brilliance manifested through austerity. Later, you attained the state of Brahmarshi. Therefore, you are worthy of being honored by me in many ways.”

Verse 56

तदद्भुतमभूद्विप्र पवित्रं परमं मम । शुभक्षेत्रगतो चाहं तव संदर्शनात्प्रभो ॥

“O Brahman, your arrival here is wondrous and supremely sacred to me. O Lord, by seeing you I have attained a sacred place.”

Verse 57

ब्रूहि यत्प्रार्थितं तुभ्यं कार्यमागमनं प्रति । इच्छाम्यनुगृहीतोऽहं त्वदर्थपरिवृद्धये । कार्यस्य न विमर्शं च गन्तुमर्हसि सुव्रत ॥

“Tell me what work you have come here seeking to accomplish. Favored by you, I wish to bring about its fulfillment for your sake. O sage of excellent vows, you should not hesitate or deliberate about whether the task can be accomplished.”

Verse 58

कर्ता चाहमशेषेण दैवतं हि भवान् मम । मम चायमनुप्राप्तो महानभ्युदयो द्विज । तवागमनजः कृत्स्नो धर्मश्चानुत्तमो मम । इति हृदयसुखं निशम्य वाक्यं श्रुतिसुखमात्मवता विनीतमुक्तम् । प्रथितगुणयशा गुणैर्विशिष्टः परमऋषिः परमं जगाम हर्षम् ॥

“I shall carry out the work completely, for you are indeed like a deity to me. O Brahman, this great prosperity has come to me, and the entire unsurpassed merit has come to me through your arrival.”

Hearing these words, spoken humbly by the wise king and pleasing both to the heart and to the ear, the great sage Vishvamitra, renowned for his qualities and distinguished by his virtues, became exceedingly delighted.

इत्यार्षे श्रीमद्रामायणे वाल्मीकीये आदिकाव्ये बालकाण्डे अष्टादशः सर्गः ॥

Thus ends the eighteenth Sarga of Bala Kanda of the Valmiki Ramayana, the first epic composed by Sage Valmiki.
