Shloka 18:1

अर्जुन उवाच -
संन्यासस्य महाबाहो तत्त्वमिच्छामि वेदितुम् ।
त्यागस्य च हृषीकेश पृथक्केशिनिषूदन ॥ १८:१ ॥

arjuna uvacha
sannyasasya maha-baho tattvam icchami veditum
tyagasya cha hrishikesha prithak keshi-nishudana

Translation

Arjuna said: O mighty-armed Krishna, I desire to understand the truth of Sannyasa (renunciation) and also of Tyaga (relinquishment), O Hrishikesha, O slayer of Keshi.

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Arjuna said to Shri Krishna, “O mighty-armed Krishna, I wish to understand the true meaning of Sannyasa (renunciation) and Tyaga (abandonment), O Hrishikesha, O slayer of Keshi.”

Kedi: Was Lord Shri Krishna a killer? Who was Keshi, and why was Keshi killed?

Ganesh: I have heard that Keshi was a demon. Whatever object or land he saw or even heard about, he sought to acquire it and expand his dominion over it. After hearing about Vrindavan, he came to conquer Vrindavan and kill its lord, Shri Krishna. Shri Krishna entered Keshi’s domain and began expanding His own domain within it, and in the end, Keshi’s existence came to an end. Lord Krishna ended the existence of Keshi, and therefore Shri Krishna came to be known as Keshava.

Kedi: You said, “I have heard.” Does that mean you are not certain about it?

Ganesh: I was not there at that time, and besides, this event was translated into Sanskrit. In reality, Keshi’s original name was something else.

Kedi: Why is Arjuna asking about Sannyasa and Tyaga in the middle of a war? What are Sannyasa and Tyaga?

Ganesh: Thoughts of Sannyasa and Tyaga arise during times of struggle. Sannyasa means giving up the desire for further experience after having had sufficient experience of something. Tyaga means giving up the desire to experience something even a little, despite never having had sufficient experience of it or no experience at all.

Kedi: I didn’t understand.

Ganesh: You work in a company that has many departments. If you leave a department after gaining sufficient experience in it, that would be your Sannyasa. If you have not gained sufficient experience in a department, or have no experience in it at all, yet you permanently refuse to work in that department, that would be your Tyaga.

Shloka 18:2

श्रीभगवानुवाच -
काम्यानां कर्मणां न्यासं संन्यासं कवयो विदुः ।
सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं प्राहुस्त्यागं विचक्षणाः ॥ १८:२ ॥

shri-bhagavan uvacha
kamyanam karmanam nyasam sannyasasya kavayo viduh
sarva-karma-phala-tyagam prahus tyagam vicakshanah

Translation

The Blessed Lord said: The wise understand Sannyasa as the renunciation of actions performed for selfish desires, while the learned declare Tyaga to be the renunciation of the fruits of all actions.

Commentary

Kedi: What does this verse of the Bhagavad Gita mean?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna tells Arjuna that wise people understand sannyasa as the renunciation of actions performed out of selfish desires, while the wise understand tyaga as the renunciation of the fruits of all actions.

Kedi: What is the renunciation of actions and the renunciation of their fruits?

Ganesh: Deciding not to perform an action that is done with a selfish motive is the renunciation of action. Giving up the expectation of a specific result from an action is the renunciation of its fruit.

Kedi: I’m confused again. Explain them one at a time. For now, just explain the renunciation of action.

Ganesh: Every human being performs actions with the desire to gain something. But when a person, despite having a strong desire, decides not to perform an action that could fulfill that desire, they consider that renunciation of action to be sannyasa.

Kedi: What is the renunciation of the fruit of action?

Ganesh: Every human being expects some result from the actions they perform, and that is natural. However, continuously expecting a particular result even before completing the action is not appropriate. Giving up that expectation is what they consider sannyasa.

Kedi: Are understanding sannyasa and actually being a sannyasi two different things?

Ganesh: Yes. Understanding something and actually becoming it are not always the same. Every person’s mindset is different, so everyone sees sannyasa from a different perspective.

Kedi: How does your mindset understand sannyasa?

Ganesh: Sannyasa has only one meaning: when a person has sufficiently experienced or attained an action or its fruit and no longer desires to experience or attain it again, or when that desire naturally disappears, that is sannyasa.

Kedi: You are saying that sannyasa means leaving something after gaining experience, and tyaga means leaving something without experience. Then why does the definition of sannyasa for the renunciation of action and the renunciation of its fruits seem different?

Ganesh: Because that definition is not Lord Krishna’s definition. The wise consider the renunciation of action as sannyasa, and the learned consider the renunciation of the fruits of action as sannyasa. But in reality, sannyasa is something that is left after sufficient experience.

Kedi: What is the difference between a wise person and a learned person?

Ganesh: A person who has knowledge about a subject is a wise person, and a person who has knowledge through experience of that same subject is a learned person.

Shloka 18:3

त्याज्यं दोषवदित्येके कर्म प्राहुर्मनीषिणः ।
यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यमिति चापरे ॥ १८:३ ॥

tyajyam dosha-vad ity eke karma prahur manishinah
yajna-dana-tapah-karma na tyajyam iti cha apare

Translation

Some wise thinkers declare that all actions should be abandoned as faulty, while others maintain that acts of sacrifice, charity, and austerity should never be given up.

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that some thoughtful thinkers say that all actions should be given up considering them defective, while others believe that actions such as sacrifice, charity, and penance should never be given up.

Kedi: Some thinkers are saying to leave everything, while some thinkers are saying not to leave good actions. But what should actually be done?

Ganesh: In reality, no action should be given a tag of right or wrong. Some actions change their tag based on time, place, and situation. Therefore, the decision should be made based on the present tag of the action.

Shloka 18:4

निश्चयं शृणु मे तत्र त्यागे भरतसत्तम ।
त्यागो हि पुरुषव्याघ्र त्रिविधः सम्प्रकीर्तितः ॥ १८:४ ॥

nishchayam shrinu me tatra tyage bharata-sattama
tyago hi purusha-vyaghra tri-vidhah samprakirtitah

Translation

Hear My definite conclusion regarding renunciation, O best of the Bharatas; for renunciation has been declared to be of three kinds.

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna, “O best of the Bharatas! Listen to My definite conclusion regarding renunciation, because renunciation has been described as being of three types.”

Kedi: Three types of renunciation means renunciation is also sattvic, rajasic, and tamasic?

Ganesh: Have patience. Lord Krishna is explaining it. Listen to the whole thing first.

Shloka 18:5

यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यं कार्यमेव तत् ।
यज्ञो दानं तपश्चैव पावनानि मनीषिणाम् ॥ १८:५ ॥

yajna-dana-tapah-karma na tyajyam karyam eva tat
yajno danam tapas chaiva pavanani manishinam

Translation

Acts of sacrifice, charity, and austerity should never be abandoned; they must indeed be performed, for they purify even the wise.

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that actions such as renunciation, charity, and penance should never be abandoned; they must certainly be performed because they purify even intelligent people.

Kedi: What are renunciation, charity, and penance?

Ganesh: Deciding not to obtain unnecessary things and deciding not to perform unnecessary actions is renunciation. Giving the unnecessary things that you have to others for free is charity, and continuously practicing to obtain something is penance.

Kedi: How can we know which action is unnecessary and which is necessary?

Ganesh: If you want to become a doctor but you are studying engineering, then studying engineering is unnecessary, even if it is free. In the same way, for someone who wants to become an engineer, studying to become a doctor is unnecessary.

Kedi: Can’t we study both medicine and engineering at the same time? Should we study only one subject?

Ganesh: If you want to live the lives of two people in one life, you can do it, but then you will also have to live the problems of two people in the same one life.

Shloka 18:6

एतान्यपि तु कर्माणि सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा फलानि च ।
कर्तव्यानीति मे पार्थ निश्चितं मतमुत्तमम् ॥ १८:६ ॥

etany api tu karmani sangam tyaktva phalani cha
kartavyaniti me partha nishchitam matam uttamam

Translation

Even these actions should be performed, O Partha, giving up attachment and the desire for their fruits. This is My firm and highest conclusion.

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna, “O Partha! These actions should also be performed only by giving up attachment to them and the desire for their fruits. This is My firm and highest conclusion.”

Kedi: What is the renunciation of attachment?

Ganesh: Suppose you have written a research paper and the world has accepted it as well. Now, if you become attached to that one research paper and keep talking only about it, you will never be able to write another research paper. In the same way, after performing any action, you should not remain attached to that action or to its fruit. If there is attachment, then you have to give up that attachment.

Shloka 18:7

नियतस्य तु संन्यासः कर्मणो नोपपद्यते ।
मोहात्तस्य परित्यागस्तामसः परिकीर्तितः ॥ १८:७ ॥

niyatasya tu sannyasah karmano nopapadyate
mohat tasya parityagas tamasah parikirtitah

Translation

The renunciation of prescribed duty is not proper. Abandoning it out of delusion is declared to be Tamasic (born of ignorance).

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that giving up one’s prescribed duty is not appropriate. Giving it up out of confusion is considered tamasic, meaning it arises from ignorance.

Kedi: What is a prescribed duty?

Ganesh: If you are an advocate, writing drafts on time is your prescribed duty. If you are a teacher, taking your class at the scheduled time is your prescribed duty. If you are a cook, preparing food at the scheduled time is your prescribed duty. The person who gives up that duty is considered tamasic.

Shloka 18:8

दुःखमित्येव यत्कर्म कायक्लेशभयात्त्यजेत् ।
स कृत्वा राजसं त्यागं नैव त्यागफलं लभेत् ॥ १८:८ ॥

duhkham ity eva yat karma kaya-klesha-bhayat tyajet
sa kritva rajasam tyagam naiva tyaga-phalam labhet

Translation

Whoever abandons prescribed duty because it is difficult or out of fear of bodily discomfort performs Rajasic renunciation and does not obtain the true fruit of renunciation.

Commentary

Kedi: What is the meaning of this verse of the Bhagavad Gita?

Ganesh: Lord Krishna is telling Arjuna that whoever gives up a prescribed duty because it is difficult or because of fear of physical suffering performs rajasic renunciation and does not receive the true fruit of renunciation.

Kedi: Is it necessary to perform actions by causing pain to the body?

Ganesh: The body is given to perform actions. Giving up an action just to keep the body comfortable is rajasic renunciation. Because of this, even when a person has the skill and ability to perform that action, they do not receive the fruit that comes from performing it, because the action never takes place.

Shloka 18:9

कार्यमित्येव यत्कर्म नियतं क्रियतेऽर्जुन ।
सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा फलं चैव स त्यागः सात्त्विको मतः ॥ १८:९ ॥

karyam ity eva yat karma niyatam kriyate ‘rjuna
sangam tyaktva phalam chaiva sa tyagah sattviko matah

Translation

When prescribed duty is performed simply because it ought to be done, abandoning attachment and the desire for its fruits, that renunciation is regarded as Sattvic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:10

न द्वेष्ट्यकुशलं कर्म कुशले नानुषज्जते ।
त्यागी सत्त्वसमाविष्टो मेधावी छिन्नसंशयः ॥ १८:१० ॥

na dveshty akushalam karma kushale nanushajjate
tyagi sattva-samavishto medhavi chhinna-samshayah

Translation

One who has truly renounced is filled with Sattva, wise, and free from doubt. Such a person neither hates unpleasant work nor becomes attached to pleasant work.

Commentary

Shloka 18:11

न हि देहभृता शक्यं त्यक्तुं कर्माण्यशेषतः ।
यस्तु कर्मफलत्यागी स त्यागीत्यभिधीयते ॥ १८:११ ॥

na hi deha-bhrita shakyam tyaktum karmany asheshatah
yas tu karma-phala-tyagi sa tyagity abhidhiyate

Translation

It is impossible for an embodied being to completely abandon all actions. But one who renounces the fruits of actions is truly called a renunciate.

Commentary

Shloka 18:12

अनिष्टमिष्टं मिश्रं च त्रिविधं कर्मणः फलम् ।
भवत्यत्यागिनां प्रेत्य न तु संन्यासिनां क्वचित् ॥ १८:१२ ॥

anishtam ishtam mishram cha tri-vidham karmanah phalam
bhavaty atyaginam pretya na tu sannyasinam kvachit

Translation

The threefold results of action—undesirable, desirable, and mixed—accrue after death to those who do not renounce the fruits of action, but never to those who have truly renounced.

Commentary

Shloka 18:13

पञ्चैतानि महाबाहो कारणानि निबोध मे ।
सांख्ये कृतान्ते प्रोक्तानि सिद्धये सर्वकर्मणाम् ॥ १८:१३ ॥

pancha etani maha-baho karanani nibodha me
sankhye kritante proktani siddhaye sarva-karmanam

Translation

O mighty-armed Arjuna, learn from Me the five causes that have been declared in the philosophy of Sankhya for the accomplishment of all actions.

Commentary

Shloka 18:14

अधिष्ठानं तथा कर्ता करणं च पृथग्विधम् ।
विविधाश्च पृथक्चेष्टा दैवं चैवात्र पञ्चमम् ॥ १८:१४ ॥

adhishthanam tatha karta karanam cha prithag-vidham
vividhash cha prithak cheshta daivam chaivatra panchamam

Translation

These are the five causes: the body (the seat of action), the doer, the various senses and instruments, the different kinds of efforts, and the Divine as the fifth factor.

Commentary

Shloka 18:15

शरीरवाङ्मनोभिर्यत्कर्म प्रारभते नरः ।
न्याय्यं वा विपरीतं वा पञ्चैते तस्य हेतवः ॥ १८:१५ ॥

sharira-vang-manobhir yat karma prarabhyate narah
nyayyam va viparitam va pancha ete tasya hetavah

Translation

Whatever action a person undertakes through body, speech, or mind—whether right or wrong—these five are its causes.

Commentary

Shloka 18:16

तत्रैवं सति कर्तारमात्मानं केवलं तु यः ।
पश्यत्यकृतबुद्धित्वान्न स पश्यति दुर्मतिः ॥ १८:१६ ॥

tatraivam sati kartaram atmanam kevalam tu yah
pashyaty akrita-buddhitvan na sa pashyati durmatih

Translation

Therefore, one whose understanding is immature and who regards the self alone as the sole doer does not truly see; such a person is of mistaken intellect.

Commentary

Shloka 18:17

यस्य नाहंकृतो भावो बुद्धिर्यस्य न लिप्यते ।
हत्वाऽपि स इमाँल्लोकान्न हन्ति न निबध्यते ॥ १८:१७ ॥

hatvapi sa iman lokan na hanti na nibadhyate
yasya nahankrito bhavo buddhir yasya na lipyate

Translation

One who is free from egoism and whose intellect is unattached is neither the slayer nor bound by actions, even if such a person were to engage in battle.

Commentary

Shloka 18:18

ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं परिज्ञाता त्रिविधा कर्मचोदना ।
करणं कर्म कर्तेति त्रिविधः कर्मसंग्रहः ॥ १८:१८ ॥

jnanam jneyam parijnata tri-vidha karma-chodana
karanam karma karteti tri-vidhah karma-sangrahah

Translation

Knowledge, the object of knowledge, and the knower are the threefold incentives to action. The instrument, the action, and the doer constitute the threefold basis of action.

Commentary

Shloka 18:19

ज्ञानं कर्म च कर्ता च त्रिधैव गुणभेदतः ।
प्रोच्यते गुणसङ्ख्याने यथावच्छृणु तान्यपि ॥ १८:१९ ॥

jnanam karma cha karta cha tridhaiva guna-bhedatah
prochyate guna-sankhyane yathavat shrinu tany api

Translation

Knowledge, action, and the doer are each declared to be of three kinds according to the distinctions of the three gunas. Hear of these from Me in detail.

Commentary

Shloka 18:20

सर्वभूतेषु येनैकं भावमव्ययमीक्षते ।
अविभक्तं विभक्तेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि सात्त्विकम् ॥ १८:२० ॥

sarva-bhuteshu yenaikam bhavam avyayam ikshate
avibhaktam vibhakteshu taj jnanam viddhi sattvikam

Translation

That knowledge by which one perceives the one imperishable Reality equally present in all beings, though they appear divided, know that knowledge to be Sattvic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:21

पृथक्त्वेन तु यज्ज्ञानं नानाभावान्पृथग्विधान् ।
वेत्ति सर्वेषु भूतेषु तज्ज्ञानं विद्धि राजसम् ॥ १८:२१ ॥

prithaktvena tu yaj jnanam nana-bhavan prithag-vidhan
vetti sarveshu bhuteshu taj jnanam viddhi rajasam

Translation

The knowledge by which one perceives different beings as separate and distinct in all respects—know that knowledge to be Rajasic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:22

यत्तु कृत्स्नवदेकस्मिन्कार्ये सक्तमहैतुकम् ।
अतत्त्वार्थवदल्पं च तत्तामसमुदाहृतम् ॥ १८:२२ ॥

yat tu kritsna-vad ekasmin karye saktam ahaitukam
atattvartha-vad alpam cha tat tamasam udahritam

Translation

That knowledge which is attached to a single effect as if it were the whole, without reason, without understanding the truth, and trivial in scope—is declared to be Tamasic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:23

नियतं सङ्गरहितमरागद्वेषतः कृतम् ।
अफलप्रेप्सुना कर्म यत्तत्सात्त्विकमुच्यते ॥ १८:२३ ॥

niyatam sanga-rahitam araga-dveshatah kritam
aphala-prepsuna karma yat tat sattvikam uchyate

Translation

That action which is prescribed, performed without attachment, free from attraction and aversion, and without desire for its fruits, is called Sattvic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:24

यत्तु कामेप्सुना कर्म साहङ्कारेण वा पुनः ।
क्रियते बहुलायासं तद्राजसमुदाहृतम् ॥ १८:२४ ॥

yat tu kamepsuna karma sahankarena va punah
kriyate bahulayasam tad rajasam udahritam

Translation

That action which is performed with desire for enjoyment or with ego, and involves great strain, is declared to be Rajasic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:25

अनुबन्धं क्षयं हिंसामनपेक्ष्य च पौरुषम् ।
मोहादारभ्यते कर्म यत्तत्तामसमुच्यते ॥ १८:२५ ॥

anubandham kshayam himsam anapekshya cha paurusham
mohad arabhyate karma yat tat tamasam uchyate

Translation

That action which is undertaken out of delusion, without considering its consequences, loss, injury to others, or one’s own capability, is called Tamasic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:26

मुक्तसङ्गोऽनहंवादी धृत्युत्साहसमन्वितः ।
सिद्ध्यसिद्ध्योर्निर्विकारः कर्ता सात्त्विक उच्यते ॥ १८:२६ ॥

mukta-sango ’naham-vadi dhrity-utsaha-samanvitah
siddhy-asiddhyor nirvikarah karta sattvika uchyate

Translation

The doer who is free from attachment, free from ego, endowed with determination and enthusiasm, and remains unchanged in success and failure, is called Sattvic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:27

रागी कर्मफलप्रेप्सुर्लुब्धो हिंसात्मकोऽशुचिः ।
हर्षशोकान्वितः कर्ता राजसः परिकीर्तितः ॥ १८:२७ ॥

ragi karma-phala-prepsur lubdho himsatmako ‘shuchih
harsha-shokanvitah karta rajasah parikirtitah

Translation

The doer who is attached, desires the fruits of action, is greedy, harmful, impure, and subject to joy and sorrow is declared to be Rajasic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:28

अयुक्तः प्राकृतः स्तब्धः शठो नैष्कृतिकोऽलसः ।
विषादी दीर्घसूत्री च कर्ता तामस उच्यते ॥ १८:२८ ॥

ayuktah prakritah stabdhah shatho naishkritiko ’lasah
vishadi dirgha-sutri cha karta tamasa uchyate

Translation

The doer who is undisciplined, vulgar, arrogant, deceitful, malicious, lazy, despondent, and procrastinating is called Tamasic.

Commentary

Shloka 18:29

Shloka 18:69

न च तस्मान्मनुष्येषु कश्चिन्मे प्रियकृत्तमः ।
भविता न च मे तस्मादन्यः प्रियतरो भुवि ॥ १८:६९ ॥

na cha tasman manushyeshu kashchin me priya-krittamah
bhavita na cha me tasmad anyah priyataro bhuvi

Translation

Among all human beings, there is no one who renders dearer service to Me than such a person, nor shall there ever be anyone more beloved to Me on earth.

Commentary

Shloka 18:70

अध्येष्यते च य इमं धर्म्यं संवादमावयोः ।
ज्ञानयज्ञेन तेनाहमिष्टः स्यामिति मे मतिः ॥ १८:७० ॥

adhyeshyate cha ya imam dharmyam samvadam avayoh
jnana-yajnena tenaham ishtah syam iti me matih

Translation

And whoever studies this sacred dialogue between us, I consider that person to worship Me through the sacrifice of knowledge.

Commentary

Shloka 18:71

श्रद्धावाननसूयश्च शृणुयादपि यो नरः ।
सोऽपि मुक्तः शुभाँल्लोकान्प्राप्नुयात्पुण्यकर्मणाम् ॥ १८:७१ ॥

shraddhavan anasuyash cha shrunuyad api yo narah
so ‘pi muktah shubhan lokan prapnuyat punya-karmanam

Translation

Even a person who faithfully hears this teaching without envy shall be freed from sin and attain the blessed realms of the virtuous.

Commentary

Shloka 18:72

कच्चिदेतच्छ्रुतं पार्थ त्वयैकाग्रेण चेतसा ।
कच्चिदज्ञानसम्मोहः प्रनष्टस्ते धनञ्जय ॥ १८:७२ ॥

kachchid etach chrutam partha tvayaikagrena chetasa
kachchid ajnana-sammohah pranashtas te dhananjaya

Translation

O Partha, have you heard this with a focused mind? O Dhananjaya, has your delusion born of ignorance now been destroyed?

Commentary

Shloka 18:73

अर्जुन उवाच -
नष्टो मोहः स्मृतिर्लब्धा त्वत्प्रसादान्मयाच्युत ।
स्थितोऽस्मि गतसन्देहः करिष्ये वचनं तव ॥ १८:७३ ॥

arjuna uvacha
nashto mohah smritir labdha tvat-prasadan mayachyuta
sthito ‘smi gata-sandehah karishye vachanam tava

Translation

Arjuna said: My delusion has been destroyed, and by Your grace, O Achyuta, my memory has been restored. I stand firm, free from doubt, and I shall act according to Your word.

Commentary

Shloka 18:74

Shloka 18:74

सञ्जय उवाच -
इत्यहं वासुदेवस्य पार्थस्य च महात्मनः ।
संवादमिममश्रौषमद्भुतं रोमहर्षणम् ॥ १८:७४ ॥

sanjaya uvacha
ity aham vasudevasya parthasya cha mahatmanah
samvadam imam ashrausham adbhutam roma-harshanam

Translation

Sanjaya said: Thus I heard this wonderful and thrilling dialogue between the great-souled Vasudeva (Krishna) and Partha (Arjuna), a conversation that caused my hair to stand on end.

Commentary

Shloka 18:75

व्यासप्रसादाच्छ्रुतवानेतद्गुह्यमहं परम् ।
योगं योगेश्वरात्कृष्णात्साक्षात्कथयतः स्वयम् ॥ १८:७५ ॥

vyasa-prasadach chrutavan etad guhyam aham param
yogam yogeshvarat krishnat sakshat kathayatah svayam

Translation

By the grace of Vyasa, I heard this supreme and most confidential Yoga directly from Krishna, the Lord of Yoga, as He Himself spoke it.

Commentary

Shloka 18:76

राजन्संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य संवादमिममद्भुतम् ।
केशवार्जुनयोः पुण्यं हृष्यामि च मुहुर्मुहुः ॥ १८:७६ ॥

rajan samsmritya samsmritya samvadam imam adbhutam
keshavarjunayoh punyam hrishyami cha muhur muhuh

Translation

O King, as I repeatedly remember this wonderful and sacred dialogue between Krishna and Arjuna, I rejoice again and again.

Commentary

Shloka 18:77

तच्च संस्मृत्य संस्मृत्य रूपमत्यद्भुतं हरेः ।
विस्मयो मे महान्राजन्हृष्यामि च पुनः पुनः ॥ १८:७७ ॥

tach cha samsmritya samsmritya rupam atyadbhutam hareh
vismayo me mahan rajan hrishyami cha punah punah

Translation

And remembering again and again the most astonishing universal form of Hari, O King, great wonder fills me, and I rejoice again and again.

Commentary

Shloka 18:78

यत्र योगेश्वरः कृष्णो यत्र पार्थो धनुर्धरः ।
तत्र श्रीर्विजयो भूतिर्ध्रुवा नीतिर्मतिर्मम ॥ १८:७८ ॥

yatra yogeshvarah krishno yatra partho dhanur-dharah
tatra shrir vijayo bhutir dhruva nitir matir mama

Translation

Wherever there is Krishna, the Lord of Yoga, and wherever there is Arjuna, the master archer, there surely abide prosperity, victory, righteousness, and enduring success. Such is my firm conviction.
